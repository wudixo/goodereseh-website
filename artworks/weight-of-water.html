II of IV Beyond the Seen

The Weight of Water

“A tear that has gathered and will not move. The kind you carry in a room full of people.”

What you see is yours alone.
No other viewer will see exactly this.

The head turns — or perhaps it has always been turned this way, slightly, away from something it cannot face or toward something it has always faced alone.

On the left, a profile emerges: the arc of a forehead, the slope of a nose, the gentle close of an eye. A single teardrop sits at its inner corner. It has not fallen. It will not fall. It is held in the precise moment between feeling and release — the most human moment there is.

On the right, a second eye rests, further away, calmer. Together they make a face that is almost a face. A person who is almost a person. Yours to complete.

The Weight of Water is about grief in its most honest form — not the dramatic grief of open weeping, but the suspended grief of a tear that has gathered and will not move. The kind you carry in a room full of people. The kind that belongs to someone specific, who is not here.

Whoever is not here — they are here, in this drawing, waiting for you to recognise them.
