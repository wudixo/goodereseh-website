The Practice

Drawing from
the human story.

Good Ereseh is a hyperrealistic charcoal and graphite artist based in London. The work focuses on the human face; the details that make a person recognisable, the emotion that lives beneath the surface.

Each artwork begins with sustained observation. The drawing process is slow by design: layers of charcoal or graphite built up over days or weeks, erased and rebuilt, until the marks on the paper begin to carry what the eye sees.

The medium is traditional. There are no digital tools, no shortcuts, no filters. What you see in the work is what the hand made; mark by mark, on heavy cotton paper, in real time.

The Medium

Charcoal.
Graphite.
Cotton paper.

Charcoal is a difficult medium. It smudges, it lifts, it doesn't stay where you put it. That's precisely what makes it right for this work. The instability of the material forces a different kind of attention, you can't be passive. You have to be present.

Graphite is the opposite: precise, resistant, unyielding. When used together, the two materials create a tonal range that neither achieves alone. The darkest darks come from compressed charcoal; the finest detail from hard graphite pencils; everything in between from careful layering and selective erasure.

The surface is always heavy cotton paper, 300gsm or above. Cotton rag has a tooth that holds charcoal in a way wood-pulp paper never does. It also ages well, remaining stable for decades without yellowing. These are works made to last.

I.
Cotton
Paper

Heavyweight archival cotton rag. The tooth and texture become part of the work, not a surface but a collaborator.

III.

IV.

No digital intervention at any stage. Every mark is a real-time decision made by a human hand.
